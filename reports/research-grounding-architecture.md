# Architecture capacity for a sub-350M grounding head

What SHAPE a network must be to represent "is this claim supported by this chunk" under a hard 350M-parameter cap, with wall-clock latency as a co-primary objective. Not a model survey (see `research-verifier-models.md`) and not a training recipe (see `research-verifier-training.md`).

- **Verdict** - architecture is almost certainly NOT the binding constraint at 350M; the incumbent's own verdict stage is a 278M encoder, and 0.80-0.84 macro-F1 is reachable by shape alone
- **The cap is spent wrong by default** - `mmBERT-base` puts 196.6M of 307M (64%) into an embedding table serving 1,800 languages; we serve 21
- **Embeddings are latency-free** - a vocabulary lookup costs ~0 FLOPs, so under a fixed parameter cap vocabulary is the only lever that improves quality AND latency at once
- **Depth is the latency enemy** - measured in-house at matched parameters, 53 layers at hidden 512 runs 5.0x slower eager and 1.58x slower CUDA-graphed than 8 layers at hidden 1536, for identical FLOPs
- **Attention-only (SAN) is a parameter lever, not a speed lever** - measured at matched parameters it uses 18% MORE FLOPs and runs 1.16-1.30x slower
- **Recommended** - 16 layers, hidden 1024, GeGLU 1536, parallel attention+FFN blocks, 128k vocabulary trimmed to the 21 served languages → 274.9M total, 2.71 ms per claim at batch-3 × 512 tokens

All latency figures below marked "measured" come from `logs/shape-bench.log` and `logs/shape-bench2.log`, generated by this investigation on the project's own RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell in bf16. They are shape stand-ins with random weights - they measure the architecture's wall-clock, not its accuracy.

## The parameter budget is a vocabulary decision

Embedding parameters are `V × d`. At 250k × 768 the table alone is 192.0M, which is 55% of a 350M cap before a single layer exists.

**Embedding table size in millions of parameters:**

| vocabulary | d=384 | d=512 | d=768 | d=1024 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8,000 | 3.1 | 4.1 | 6.1 | 8.2 |
| 16,000 | 6.1 | 8.2 | 12.3 | 16.4 |
| 32,000 | 12.3 | 16.4 | 24.6 | 32.8 |
| 64,000 | 24.6 | 32.8 | 49.2 | 65.5 |
| 128,000 | 49.2 | 65.5 | 98.3 | 131.1 |
| 250,000 | 96.0 | 128.0 | 192.0 | 256.0 |

**Layers affordable under a 350M cap** (GLU block, `d_ff ≈ 8/3 · d`, per-layer cost `4d² + 3·d·d_ff`):

| vocabulary | d=384 (1.77M/layer) | d=512 (3.11M/layer) | d=768 (7.08M/layer) | d=1024 (12.65M/layer) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8,000 | 196 | 111 | 48.6 | 27.0 |
| 32,000 | 191 | 107 | 46.0 | 25.1 |
| 64,000 | 184 | 102 | 42.5 | 22.5 |
| 128,000 | 170 | 91 | 35.6 | 17.3 |
| 250,000 | 143 | 71 | 22.3 | 7.4 |

The bottom-right cell is the trap: at 250k × 1024 the cap buys 7.4 layers. The top-left is the opposite trap - 196 layers at hidden 384 is arithmetically legal and would be the slowest model in this report by a wide margin.

### Tied, untied and factorised

- **Tied vs untied is moot at serving** - a pairwise classifier has no output embedding; the MLM head is discarded after pretraining, so the tying decision affects pretraining only and never the deployed parameter count
- **Factorised (ALBERT-style)** - project `V → E → d`; cost falls from `V·d` to `V·E + E·d`. At V=128k, d=1024, E=256 that is 33.6M against 131.1M, a 97.5M saving
- **ALBERT's own ablation** - E=768 beats E=128 by "no more than 1% in absolute values"; under full parameter sharing E=128 was best, and the all-shared penalty was -1.5 average at E=128 against -2.5 at E=768 ([ALBERT, arXiv 1909.11942](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.11942))
- **But factorisation is not needed here** - it trades quality for parameters we do not need to save, since the recommended body is only 143M; use it only if the vocabulary must exceed 200k
- **Parameter sharing is a different question** - ALBERT-base is 11.0M total because all 12 layers are one layer reused, and it costs full FLOPs (96.6 GFLOP at 512 tokens, the same as ELECTRA-base at 108M) for a measurable accuracy loss - sharing helps memory footprint, never latency

### Smallest vocabulary that serves 21 languages

- **Tokens per language is the honest metric** - XLM-R spends 2,500 slots/language over 100; mBERT 1,150 over 104; Gemma2 (mmBERT) 142 over 1,800
- **At 21 languages a 64k vocabulary gives 3,000 slots/language and 128k gives 6,100** - both above XLM-R's density, so fertility on the 21 served languages should be BETTER than the incumbent's 250k table, not worse
- **In-house upper bound on the cost** - R4-H22 measured an 8,192-token English-only SYNTH BPE at 2.342 tok/word against mDeBERTa's 1.959, ratio **1.196**, at 30x fewer slots; that is the worst case, English-only and 30x smaller
- **Measured latency price of fertility** - +25% tokens (512 → 640) costs +18% wall-clock (2.769 → 3.263 ms CUDA-graphed), so the 1.196 worst case would cost ~15%
- **A domain-specific BPE buys parameters, not speed** - and if the vocabulary is targeted at our language set rather than shrunk, it buys both, because shorter sequences are the dominant latency term
- **Build path** - prefer trimming an existing 250k/256k table to the 21-language token set over training a fresh BPE; trimming preserves pretrained weights, and [Load What You Need (Abdaoui et al., SustaiNLP 2020)](https://aclanthology.org/2020.sustainlp-1.16/) removed up to 45% of mBERT's parameters with comparable results, while distillation cost 1.7-6% on XNLI; [vocabulary trimming (Ushio et al., 2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15020) reports 39% compression at no NLI loss on XLM-R

### The counter-intuitive consequence

Embedding lookups are a gather, not a matmul. Serving FLOPs are `≈ 2 · body_params · tokens`, independent of `V`.

- **Cutting the vocabulary does not make the model faster** - it frees cap that, if spent on layers, makes it slower
- **Under a latency objective, max the vocabulary and keep the body small** - the opposite of the instinct the 350M cap creates
- **`mmBERT-base` is already near this optimum by accident** - 196.6M embedding / 110.3M body is a latency-cheap split; its flaw is that the 196.6M serves 1,800 languages instead of 21

Readings:

- **Only four in-band backbones are genuinely multilingual** - mDeBERTa-v3-base, XLM-R-base, mmBERT-base/small, EuroBERT-210m; all four spend 46-70% of their parameters on vocabulary
- **NeoBERT is the anti-pattern for latency** - 11% embedding, 198M body, 225.5 GFLOP per forward, the slowest in-band model measured (8.64 ms eager); its English-only 30,522 vocabulary means it also cannot serve the task
- **DeBERTa-v3's disentangled attention triples the attention term** - content-to-content plus content-to-position plus position-to-content; this is why the incumbent NLI stage is not as cheap as its 85M body suggests
- **512 tokens is enough** - R4-H22 measured EN evidence at median 438 / p95 553 tokens for a 1,500-char chunk; ModernBERT's 8,192 context buys nothing because max-over-chunks already works and attention is quadratic, measured at 512 → 1024 costing 2.25x (2.769 → 6.228 ms)

## Depth versus width, on both axes

The two axes disagree, and the disagreement is the whole design decision.

### Quality per parameter favours depth, but with saturation and a domain caveat

- **MobileLLM ([arXiv 2402.14905](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14905))** at 125M: 4L/1280d 43.3%, 12L/768d 43.9%, 30L/512d 44.8%, 62L/384d 44.7% - depth pays +0.9 points from 12 to 30 layers then **saturates and slightly reverses**
- **MobileLLM at 350M**: 5L/2048d 47.1%, 15L/1280d 48.7%, 32L/896d 49.8% - still climbing at 32 layers, +1.1 from 15L
- **NeoBERT ([arXiv 2502.19587](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.19587))** rebalanced 16×1056 → 28×768 on exactly this argument, calling BERT-class models "width-inefficient"
- **Levine et al. ([arXiv 2006.12467](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.12467))** - optimal depth is **logarithmic** in network size, there is a width-dependent transition, and "a deeper self-attention network can even underperform relative to a shallower one, when its width is too small"; they conclude GPT-3 is too deep for its size
- **The domain caveat that voids most of this for us** - every MobileLLM benchmark (TQA, RACE, common-sense QA) is parametric-recall-heavy, which is precisely the axis the SAN controlled study localises the FFN/depth benefit to, and precisely the axis a grounding task never exercises because it is handed its evidence
- **Counter-evidence on encoders** - [Wide Attention Is The Way Forward For Transformers? (arXiv 2210.00640)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.00640) trained wide single-layer encoders from scratch on 4 NLP tasks × 10 attention types and found them **+0.3% better on average** than deep counterparts, at half the size and 3.1x faster CPU inference at equal accuracy
- **Pleias Monad/Baguettotron (64L @ 256, 80L @ 576)** is a vendor blog with no peer-reviewed paper, and R6-H40 could not reproduce Monad's published MMLU (0.255/0.226/0.143 against a published 0.30, chance 0.25) - treat the depth-over-width claim from that line as unvalidated

### Wall-clock strictly favours width, and the effect is large

Measured here, batch 3 × 512 tokens, bf16, matched body parameters (~143-160M) and near-matched FLOPs:

| shape | L | d | body M | GFLOP | eager ms | graph ms | achieved TFLOP/s |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| narrow-deep | 53 | 512 | 149.5 | 544.2 | 14.909 | 4.438 | 122.6 |
| deep | 28 | 768 | 140.5 | 498.9 | 8.643 | 3.330 | 149.8 |
| balanced | 16 | 1024 | 142.7 | 489.6 | 4.686 | 2.806 | 174.5 |
| wide-shallow | 8 | 1536 | 160.5 | 531.5 | 2.976 | 2.813 | 188.9 |
| wider | 12 | 1280 | 167.2 | 561.7 | 3.241 | 2.788 | 180.2 |
| attention-only (SAN) | 36 | 1024 | 151.1 | 579.8 | 6.087 | 3.246 | 178.6 |

- **Eager: 5.0x penalty for narrow-deep** at identical parameters and FLOPs, because 53 layers is 53 sequential dependency points and ~500 kernel launches
- **CUDA-graphed: 1.58x penalty survives** even with launch overhead eliminated, because achieved throughput falls from 188.9 to 122.6 TFLOP/s - a 512-wide GEMM under-fills the tensor cores
- **Width buys utilisation, not just parallelism** - this is the part the FLOP count cannot see
- **The penalty is worst at small batch, which is our regime** - at batch 8 the narrow-deep gap compresses from 1.58x to 1.38x (10.316 vs 7.469 ms); serving three chunks is the worst case for depth

### Resolution

The two axes disagree, so state the trade rather than hide it: **depth buys roughly +1 point of quality-per-parameter on parametric-recall tasks and costs 1.6-5.0x wall-clock; grounding does not use the axis depth buys.** Take the width. Do not take it to the extreme - 8 layers at hidden 1536 matches MobileLLM's worst-performing 350M arm in shape, and Levine warns the too-wide regime is also suboptimal. **16 layers at hidden 1024 is the defensible middle**: 1.19x faster graphed and 1.84x faster eager than 28×768, at the same parameters, and well inside the plateau MobileLLM measured.

## FFN versus attention-only

The SAN study ([arXiv 2607.18363](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18363)) localises the entire attention-only deficit to parametric recall and measures SANs AHEAD on passage-supplied answers (Sciq 0.725 → 0.742 while the FFN arm regressed 0.702 → 0.661). The mechanism argument transfers to grounding. The engineering does not.

- **Iso-parameter arithmetic** - a standard layer holds `12d²` (4d² attention + 8d² FFN), an attention-only layer `4d²`, so matched parameters means **3x the layers**
- **FLOP consequence** - matmul FLOPs are identical by construction, but the quadratic attention term triples: `(24Td² + 12T²d) / (24Td² + 4T²d)` = **1.20x total FLOPs at T=512, d=768**
- **Measured** - 36L attention-only at hidden 1024 (151.1M body) runs 579.8 GFLOP / 6.087 ms eager / 3.246 ms graphed against 16L with FFN (142.7M body) at 489.6 GFLOP / 4.686 / 2.806: **18% more FLOPs, 1.30x slower eager, 1.16x slower graphed**
- **The pre-existing critique holds** - implementing FFN-equivalent computation via attention is more expensive than an FFN, and requires a much larger `d_model` to compensate
- **Transfer to a pairwise encoder is untested** - SAN is decoder-only, next-token loss, 6-87M parameters, one vendor (Cactus Compute, which ships the derived Needle model), no independent replication found as of July 2026; the iso-param seed pairs agree to 0.0055 nats but one of three had a documented terminal instability that reverses sign
- **Verdict** - **keep the FFN**. Attention-only trades wall-clock for parameters, and parameters are the resource we have spare (274.9M of a 350M cap) while wall-clock is the one we do not. Carry forward the one transferable SAN finding: **QK-normalization**, which the paper identifies as the trainability precondition for deep attention stacks and which ViT-22B independently adopted for stability at width

## Parallel residual streams and the parallel-block question

The hypothesis under test: at fixed FLOPs, buy capacity through parallel work rather than sequential layers. It is directionally right. Its three candidate mechanisms are not equally supported.

### 1. Hyper-Connections - real, cheap, but not a latency mechanism

[Hyper-Connections (arXiv 2409.19606, ByteDance Seed, ICLR 2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.19606) confirmed. Expands the single residual stream into `n` parallel streams with learned depth-connections and width-connections; static (SHC) and dynamic input-dependent (DHC) variants; expansion rates n ∈ {1,2,4,8} tested.

- **Parameter overhead is negligible** - `d_model·(n+2) + n·(n+2) + 2` per layer; +0.033% on OLMo-1B-DHC×4, +0.023% on OLMo-7B
- **FLOP overhead is negligible** - +0.147% to +0.208% forward FLOPs
- **Activation memory overhead is NOT negligible** - +26.1% on OLMo-1B, +28.3% on OLMo-7B, +9.7% on OLMoE
- **Quality gains** - OLMo-1B +1.3pp downstream average (62.5 → 63.8), OLMo-7B +0.9pp, OLMoE-1B-7B converged 1.8x faster with ARC-Challenge +6 points
- **Encoder evidence exists** - ViT-B/16 ImageNet classification 76.38 → 77.60 (SHC), ViT-L/16 77.25 → 79.94 (DHC); DiT-XL/2-SHC×2 matched a DiT with **50% more parameters**. These are bidirectional encoders on a classification objective, which is the closest published analogue to our task
- **n=1 is worse than baseline; gains diminish beyond n=4**
- **Correction to the stated mechanism** - hyper-connections do NOT compute `n` streams as concurrent branches of work. They widen the residual STATE to `n×d` and let the same, still strictly sequential, layer stack read and write a learned mixture of it. The critical path is unchanged. HC is a **capacity-per-layer lever at near-zero FLOPs**, not a way to avoid the sequential wait
- **Replication status - qualified** - not independently reproduced in the strict sense. DeepSeek adopted and extended it as [mHC (arXiv 2512.24880)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.24880) for DeepSeek-V4, with follow-ups KromHC (2601.21579) and go-mHC (2604.02309). Different-organisation adoption is stronger than nothing but is not a replication of the original numbers
- **mHC's abstract is the warning label**, verbatim: diversifying connectivity "fundamentally compromises the identity mapping property intrinsic to the residual connection, which causes severe training instability and restricted scalability, and additionally incurs notable memory access overhead"
- **Nobody has tested HC on a text encoder or on pairwise classification**
- **Verdict** - include **n=2 static (SHC)** as an ablatable switch with a pre-registered kill rule, not as a load-bearing element. It is the cheapest variant, it is the one that produced the ViT-Base gain, and n=2 halves the memory overhead relative to the ×4 configurations. If it fails to reproduce on our task in one ablation, remove it and lose nothing

### 2. Parallel attention + FFN blocks - proven, including on encoders

Compute attention and FFN from the same normed input and sum, rather than sequentially. The strongest-supported of the three.

- **He & Hofmann, [Simplifying Transformer Blocks (arXiv 2311.01906, ICLR 2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.01906)** - parallel MHA/MLP plus a simplified attention sub-block gave **16% faster training throughput with 15% fewer parameters** at matched per-update convergence, verified on **BERT encoder-only** as well as decoder-only. This is the encoder evidence the SAN and HC lines lack
- **ViT-22B ([arXiv 2302.05442](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05442))** - an encoder, uses parallel blocks and explicitly fuses the QKV projection with the FFN's first linear into one matmul, and the attention out-projection with the FFN's second
- **PaLM** - 15% training speedup, small quality degradation at 8B, none at 62B; 14% higher inference latency for the serial form
- **Honest scoping** - PaLM's and ViT-22B's gains are substantially a tensor-parallel communication reduction (two all-reduces per layer become one), which does not apply on one device
- **Measured here on one device, unfused** - -5% to -11% eager, -2% to -3% graphed. Modest. The larger share of the published gain comes from the GEMM fusion, which my stand-in did not implement; fusing `1024 → 3·1024 + 2·1536 = 6144` into one GEMM raises arithmetic intensity and is the part worth building
- **Verdict** - **adopt**. It halves the sequential sub-block count (2L → L dependency points), it is proven on encoders by an independent academic group, and the fused-GEMM form is exactly the "parallel work in one pass" the hypothesis asks for

### 3. Batching the chunks - the biggest parallel-work win, and it is free

- **Measured** - one forward at batch 1 costs 1.329 ms graphed; batch 3 costs 2.727 ms, not 3.987 ms
- **Batching the top-3 chunks as one forward instead of three sequential forwards saves 32%** at zero architectural cost
- This is the parallel-versus-sequential principle applied where it actually pays, and it requires only that the architecture accept a fixed `[3, 512]` input shape

### Other multi-stream work

- **DenseFormer, ResiDual, multi-branch transformers** - all add cross-layer or cross-branch mixing; none has encoder pairwise-classification evidence, and none reported a wall-clock win. No reason to spend a round on them over the two mechanisms above
- **CQIL ([arXiv 2404.06709](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06709))** - runs quasi-independent layers concurrently for inference latency; decoder-only, post-hoc surgery on a trained model, not an architecture to pretrain

### Answer to the hypothesis

At a matched sub-350M budget, a wide-shallow cross-encoder beats a narrow-deep one on wall-clock decisively (measured 1.19-5.0x depending on capture mode and how narrow) and loses about 1 point of quality-per-parameter on tasks that need parametric recall, which grounding does not. Parallel residual streams are real and cheap but are a capacity lever, not a latency lever, and are unproven on text encoders. **Take the width, take the parallel blocks, take the batching, and treat hyper-connections as a cheap ablation.**

## Interaction pattern

What our own measurements rule in and out:

- **Bi-encoder alone is refuted** - AUC 0.53-0.73 against the cross-encoder's 0.841; a single vector cannot represent "is this specific assertion present"
- **Fusing multiple chunks into one context is refuted** - macro-F1 0.714-0.784, down 0.012-0.081, and the fused NLI correlates only 0.54 with per-chunk max. R4-H22 replicated the null (joint-premise NLI, -0.002)
- **What that does NOT rule out** - it forbids packing several chunks into one forward. It says nothing about WHERE inside the network the claim and one chunk fuse
- **Late interaction (ColBERT/ColBERTv2 MaxSim) is refuted by the same evidence** - MaxSim over independently-encoded token vectors has no path to represent "the passage omits the second half of this conjunction", which is our dominant failure mode (omission, not contradiction). Index bloat of 1-2 orders of magnitude is the secondary objection
- **Token-level tagging is complementary, not alternative** - a per-token supported/unsupported head sits on top of a cross-encoder and costs one linear layer; it is how LettuceDetect and PsiloQA emit spans, and it matches our output contract. Add it as a second head, not as the interaction pattern
- **Contradiction channels add nothing** - already measured; the head is 2-way, not 3-way

**Mid-fusion is the one live alternative.** [MICE (arXiv 2602.16299)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16299) encodes query and document independently for the first N layers, then applies limited cross-attention in the top M, removing document-to-query attention and document self-attention in the interaction layers.

- **MiniLM-L12 configuration** - 4 independent + ~3 interaction layers
- **In-domain cost** - MS MARCO 43.2 vs 45.0 nDCG@10 (-1.8), TREC-DL19 73.4 vs 74.0 (-0.6)
- **Out-of-domain GAIN** - BEIR 49.7 vs 44.0 (**+5.7**), and it "substantially outperforms ColBERT across all datasets tested"
- **Reported efficiency** - 2.5x FLOP reduction, "decreases fourfold the inference latency compared to standard cross-encoders"
- **Why it fits us structurally** - the evidence chunks are indexed offline, so the independent document-side layers are precomputable exactly like the existing bi-encoder index; and because document tokens do not self-attend in the interaction layers, those layers process ~64 claim tokens instead of 512
- **Measured here** - 6 interaction layers at hidden 1024 over 64 claim tokens costs **0.298 ms** graphed against 2.707 ms for the full 16-layer 512-token forward, a **9.1x online reduction**
- **The cost** - caching one layer's activations per chunk is 512 × 1024 × 2 bytes = 1.05 MB/chunk, so a 50k-chunk corpus is ~52 GB; and the BEIR result is ranking, not entailment - delaying fusion may cost more on entailment than on relevance

## Recommended specification

**GR-Enc-275M** - a wide, moderately deep, fully-joint cross-encoder with a 21-language vocabulary.

| element | value | justification |
|---|---|---|
| family | ModernBERT / EuroBERT lineage encoder - pre-norm, RoPE, GLU FFN, SDPA/FlashAttention, unpadding | proven encoder stack, no `trust_remote_code`, clean ONNX/OpenVINO export |
| layers | **16** | measured 1.19x faster graphed / 1.84x faster eager than 28×768 at equal parameters; inside MobileLLM's plateau; avoids Levine's too-wide regime that 8 layers would enter |
| hidden | **1024** | measured achieved throughput 174.5 TFLOP/s vs 122.6 at hidden 512; 16 heads × 64 head-dim tiles cleanly on tensor cores |
| heads | 16 (head dim 64) | standard tile-friendly head dimension |
| FFN | GeGLU, `d_ff = 1536` (Wi 1024→3072, Wo 1536→1024) | keep the FFN - attention-only measured 18% more FLOPs and 1.16-1.30x slower at matched parameters |
| block form | **parallel attention + FFN**, fused input GEMM 1024→6144, fused output | halves sequential sub-block count; He & Hofmann verified on BERT encoders at 16% faster, 15% fewer parameters, matched quality |
| normalisation | pre-norm + **QK-norm** | SAN identifies QK-norm as the trainability precondition at depth; ViT-22B adopted it for stability at width |
| vocabulary | **128,000**, trimmed from a 250k/256k multilingual table to the 21 served languages | 6,100 slots/language vs XLM-R's 2,500 → fertility better than the incumbent, so sequences get shorter; trimming preserves pretraining (Load What You Need: -45% parameters, comparable results) |
| embeddings | untied, unfactorised | no output embedding exists at serving; factorisation would trade ALBERT's ~1% for parameters we do not need |
| sequence length | train 1024, **serve at fixed 512** | R4-H22: p95 evidence 553 tokens; measured 512→1024 costs 2.25x |
| interaction | **full joint cross-attention**, `[CLS] claim [SEP] chunk [SEP]`, top-3 chunks as ONE batch, max over chunks | joint attention is load-bearing by our own measurement; batching measured to save 32% over three forwards |
| heads | 2-way grounded/ungrounded on `[CLS]` + token-level supported-span head | contradiction channel adds nothing; the span head is one linear layer and matches the output contract |
| optional | n=2 **static** hyper-connections | +0.03% parameters, +0.2% FLOPs, +10-15% activation memory; ablatable, with a kill rule |

| component | arithmetic | parameters |
|---|---|---|
| token embeddings | 128,000 × 1,024 | 131.07M |
| attention, 16 layers | 16 × 4 × 1024² | 67.11M |
| GeGLU FFN, 16 layers | 16 × (2·1024·1536 + 1536·1024) | 75.50M |
| norms, pooler, 2 heads | - | ~1.2M |

| body (FLOP-bearing) | | **142.7M** |

**Latency arithmetic** - RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell, bf16, single-stream, batch 3 × 512 tokens:

- FLOPs per (claim, chunk) forward: `16 × (8·512·1024² + 4·512²·1024 + 2·512·2·1024·1536 + 2·512·1536·1024)` = **163.2 GFLOP**
- Batch of 3: **489.6 GFLOP**
- Sequential depth cost: 16 layers × 1 parallel sub-block = **16 dependency points** (against 44 for a serial 22-layer model, 106 for a serial 53-layer one)
- **Measured: 4.11 ms eager, 2.71 ms CUDA-graph captured**, 180 TFLOP/s achieved
- Batch 1 for reference: 1.33 ms graphed

**What makes it fast in practice, and what would block it:**

- **Fixed input shape `[3, 512]`** enables CUDA-graph capture and OpenVINO/ONNX static-shape compilation - measured 1.52x (4.11 → 2.71 ms). Blocked by dynamic padding, ragged batches, or any data-dependent control flow
- **Top-3 chunks as one batch** - measured 2.727 ms against 3.987 ms for three batch-1 forwards, a 32% saving. Blocked by per-chunk early exit or per-chunk thresholding inside the graph
- **Truncate to 512** - measured 512 vs 1024 is 2.25x. Blocked by adopting a long-context backbone and actually using the context
- **Width over depth** - measured 1.58-5.0x against narrow-deep. Blocked by choosing a Monad-style shape
- **Parallel blocks with fused GEMMs** - blocked by any framework that cannot fuse; keep the fusion in the exported graph, not only in the training code
- **No `trust_remote_code`, no custom attention kernels** - blocked by EuroBERT-style pinned remote code; the ModernBERT/mmBERT family's int8 pathology (attention masked-fill interacting badly with INT8 calibration) is a known export risk and needs the attention-nodes-excluded recipe

**Expected speedup over 662 ms**, and where it comes from:

**4-6x on CPU (662 ms → ~120-170 ms), or ~3 ms on one GPU** - because deleting the 568M bge-reranker removes ~87% of the online FLOPs (8 forwards × 335 GFLOP = 2,680 of ~3,065 GFLOP total), three model loads collapse to one, and twelve separate forward passes collapse to one fixed-shape batched forward.

- Incumbent online FLOPs per claim, corpus embeddings cached: bi-encoder claim encode ~37 + reranker 8 × 335 + NLI 3 × 116 = **~3,065 GFLOP**
- Recommended, retrieval unchanged: 1 batched forward = **490 GFLOP**, a **6.3x** FLOP reduction
- If the new model must also rerank 8 candidates: **1,306 GFLOP**, a 2.3x reduction - so pairing it with the existing lexical tier or a cached bi-encoder index for candidate selection is worth ~2.7x on its own
- The CPU figure is a FLOP-proportional projection and is **not measured**; the GPU figures are measured on shape stand-ins with random weights

## Named alternative

**GR-MICE-210M** - mid-fusion, 10 independent + 6 joint layers, hidden 1024, same 128k vocabulary.

- **The different bet** - that claim-chunk fusion can be delayed to the top third of the network without losing entailment quality, in exchange for precomputing the document side offline
- **Payoff** - measured 0.298 ms online against 2.707 ms, a **9.1x** reduction, because the 6 interaction layers process ~64 claim tokens rather than 512
- **Evidence for** - MICE loses only 1.8 nDCG@10 in-domain and **gains 5.7 on BEIR out-of-domain**; our cross-lingual and prose slices are effectively out-of-domain, which is where mid-fusion measured best
- **Evidence against** - MICE is ranking, not entailment; omission detection may need earlier fusion than relevance does; ~1.05 MB of cached activations per chunk means ~52 GB for a 50k-chunk corpus, and the cache invalidates on any re-chunking
- **What decides between them** - train both heads on the same slice of the cached 111,800-pair teacher matrix for one epoch and compare OOF AUC on the gold. **Take MICE if its AUC deficit against the full cross-encoder is ≤ 0.01**; the 9.1x is worth more than that. Above 0.02, take the full cross-encoder and buy speed from batching and quantisation instead

## Cheapest decisive experiment

**Capacity ablation on models that already exist - about 2.5 hours on one GPU, no pretraining, no new data.** It answers whether this task is capacity-limited at all before anyone commits to a custom backbone.

1. Fine-tune four existing checkpoints on soft labels from the cached 111,800-pair matrix, 1 epoch each, identical schedule → verify: 4 runs, ~30 min each (FactCG needed 18 min for DeBERTa-large on 4 GPUs)
   - **(a)** `mDeBERTa-v3-base` unchanged, 12L × 768, 85M body - the capacity reference
   - **(b)** the same with the top 6 layers dropped, 6L × 768, 42M body - halves depth
   - **(c)** `mmBERT-base`, 22L × 768, 110M body
   - **(d)** `mmBERT-small`, 22L × 384, 42M body - halves width
2. Score all four on the held-out 2,752 gold, macro-F1, under the Round-3 GroupKFold leave-one-source-out protocol → verify: four numbers plus four teacher-agreement numbers
3. Read the result:
   - **(b) ≈ (a)** → depth is not binding, the wide-shallow bet is safe, proceed to the recommended spec
   - **(d) ≈ (c)** → width is not binding either; the task is **not capacity-limited**, the 350M cap is slack, and the ceiling is set by data and training, not architecture - in which case do not build a custom backbone at all, spend the budget on constructed-label negatives instead
   - **(b) and (d) both fall > 0.03 below their references** → capacity IS binding, and the recommended spec's 142.7M body is the right place to spend, with the wide variant preferred on the measured latency evidence
4. The latency half is already answered and reproducible in 3 minutes - `logs/shape-bench.log`, `logs/shape-bench2.log`

This composes with the training lane's learning-curve probe (10K/40K/111K volumes) - run them as one sweep and get both answers for the price of one.

## Honest ceiling

**A sub-350M architecture can plausibly reach 0.80-0.84 macro-F1, and architecture is not what stands in the way.**

- The incumbent's own verdict stage is `mDeBERTa-v3-base` at 278M with an 85M body - the representational question was answered before this report started
- `FactCG-DeBERTa-v3-Large` at 0.4B beats gpt-4o on LLM-AggreFact; `LettuceDetect-large` at 396M beat a fine-tuned Llama-2-13B
- The risk is not capacity. It is that 0.824 is produced by **three stages seeing three different views** - bi-encoder recall, reranker selection, entailment verdict - and a single cross-encoder inherits the verdict but only inherits the selection if the aggregation and candidate set are preserved
- This lane and the training lane reached **0.78-0.84** independently, by different routes (architecture capacity here, distillation ceiling there). Convergent estimates from independent arguments are the strongest signal available
- **A clear beat of 0.824 is unlikely from architecture alone.** The payoff is one 275M forward replacing three model loads and twelve forward passes, at 4-6x lower cost

## Evidence quality

Read this before quoting any number above.

- **Measured in-house, reproducible** - every wall-clock, FLOP and achieved-throughput figure in the depth/width, attention-only, sequence-length, batching and mid-fusion tables; RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell, bf16, random weights. These measure architecture wall-clock, **not accuracy**
- **Derived by arithmetic from published configs** - every embedding/body split; validated against published totals where available (ModernBERT-base 149.0M ✓, mmBERT-base 306.9M ✓, mmBERT-small 140.5M and 42M non-embedding ✓, mDeBERTa-v3-base 277.7M ✓, EuroBERT-210m 211.7M ✓)
- **Peer-reviewed** - Levine et al. (NeurIPS 2020), Tay et al. DeepNarrow (200+ configurations, 5M-30B), MobileLLM (ICML 2024), He & Hofmann (ICLR 2024), Hyper-Connections (ICLR 2025), ALBERT, Poly-encoders (ICLR 2020), Abdaoui et al. (SustaiNLP 2020), Ushio et al. (2023)
- **Vendor-authored, flag on every citation** - SAN controlled study (Cactus Compute, which ships the derived Needle model; preprint; no independent replication found); Hyper-Connections (ByteDance Seed; extended by DeepSeek but not replicated); mHC (DeepSeek); Pleias SYNTH/Monad/Baguettotron (blog post, no paper, and **R6-H40 failed to reproduce Monad's published MMLU**)
- **Preprint, unreplicated** - MICE (2026), the load-bearing evidence for the named alternative; its numbers are ranking metrics on MS MARCO/BEIR, not entailment
- **The transfer question is open for three of the four mechanisms** - SAN is decoder-only next-token at 6-87M; hyper-connections have ViT classification evidence but no text-encoder or pairwise evidence; MICE is ranking. Only parallel blocks have direct BERT-encoder evidence (He & Hofmann). Every one of these must be measured on our own gold, not inherited
