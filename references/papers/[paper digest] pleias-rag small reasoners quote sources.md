**Even Small Reasoners Should Quote Their Sources: Introducing the Pleias-RAG Model Family (2025)**

Pleias introduce Pleias-RAG-350m and Pleias-RAG-1B, small reasoning models mid-trained for retrieval-augmented generation, search and source summarization. The problem addressed is that small models used as latent databases hallucinate in proportion to how little they can memorise, and that RAG research has largely optimised answer quality while "largely overlooking its ability to attribute sources accurately". The proposed answer is to stop treating citation as post-processing: both models generate references and literal quote excerpts inline during inference, using a Wikipedia-inspired `<ref>` syntax, and are trained with a structured reasoning sequence that includes an explicit refusal path when the supplied sources cannot answer the query. The authors reframe the model as a source reasoner "primarily conceived to work in interaction with an external memory" rather than a knowledge store. Both models outperform sub-4B SLMs on HotPotQA and 2WikiMultiHopQA and are competitive with Qwen-2.5-7B, Llama-3.1-8B and Gemma-3-4B, while being the only SLMs holding RAG performance across major European languages.

**Key mechanism**

- Mid-training, not from scratch: both are continued-training variants of Pleias 1.0 base models (trained on Common Corpus, ~2T tokens of public-domain or permissively licensed text), using the same Nanotron framework on ~10B tokens of synthetically augmented data for just under 2 epochs
- Citations are generated by the model rather than reconstructed post hoc; the authors contrast this explicitly with Anthropic's citation mode, which they reconstruct as emitting chunk anchor identifiers that are then re-injected as literal quotes. Their objection is practical - undocumented and widely varying chunk sizes make verification cumbersome, and post-hoc cross-referencing proved unreliable in production
- Generated citations permit automated shortening: the model keeps only the span relevant for grounding, using "(...)" elision, which external anchoring cannot do
- A structured reasoning rubric routes three cases - trivial queries answered without close source examination, standard queries requiring synthesis, and refusal when the query is valid but the sources cannot support an answer. The model self-determines how much inference time to allocate, which the authors call adjustable reasoning and proto-agentic
- Training data was generated by rubric engineering: 4,000 seed examples generated with Gemma 3 27B from a pool of 3 million RAG examples, iteratively filtered to remove hallucinated citations and malformed traces, then used to fine-tune a Gemma 12B base model which generated the full set at scale
- Tokenizer recycling: rather than pre-allocating untrained special tokens, the last 19 BPE tokens (least useful for fertility, least trained) are repurposed as special tokens, reducing both waste and the prompt-injection surface of untrained tokens

**Main findings**

- Pleias-RAG-350M and Pleias-RAG-1.2B are reported state of the art on 2WikiMultiHopQA for their size range and Pareto-optimal among SLMs on HotPotQA versus model size
- Competitive with models roughly 6-20x larger (Qwen-2.5-7B, Llama-3.1-8B, Gemma-3-4B) on multi-hop RAG benchmarks
- Multilingual robustness is the sharpest differentiator: sub-1B baselines degrade 10% to 30-35% when moved off English, while the Pleias models show negligible loss across French, Italian, German and Spanish
- The evaluation set deliberately includes queries whose answers are absent from the supplied sources, to measure refusal rather than only answer quality, and varies the number of sources to probe hallucination propensity
- Judged evaluation used a fine-tuned Gemma 3 12B comparing 864 answers against reference answers - an LLM-judge protocol, so the absolute numbers inherit that judge's biases
- The authors report that carefully designed synthetic reasoning traces were the dominant factor: earlier runs with less structured traces produced a stationary loss curve after a few billion tokens, while the final recipe was still improving at 16B tokens
- A learning rate about an order of magnitude above the end-of-pretraining value was needed for mid-training to move
- Explicit methodological note, well supported elsewhere: "the strongest models do not make the strongest teachers" - a fine-tuned Gemma 12B was preferred over Gemma 27B for scaled generation, on both cost and steerability grounds
- Caveats: preprint, vendor-authored, self-selected benchmark suite, LLM-as-judge scoring, and the models are designed exclusively for source synthesis so they are penalised on tasks expecting parametric answers

**Key takeaways**

- Grounding and citation can be trained into models far below 1B parameters - this is the strongest existing evidence that a small model can do source-attributed verification, and it is the closest published precedent for a tiny grounding head
- Prefer generated citations over post-hoc chunk anchoring when the deliverable is a verifiable span; the authors' production experience is that retrospective cross-referencing does not hold up, which is a direct warning to any grounder that reconstructs spans after the fact
- Build an explicit refusal path into the training objective; "the sources do not support this" must be a first-class output, not an absence of output
- Mid-training on ~10B tokens over a competent base is enough to install a structured behaviour - a far cheaper intervention than pretraining, and directly applicable to specialising a small base for grounding
- Use a mid-sized fine-tuned teacher rather than the largest available model for scaled synthetic generation; steerability beats raw capability for format-constrained output
- Rubric-engineered reasoning traces with fixed steps outperformed free-form traces on a formal task - relevant to any decision about whether a grounding model should emit a trace and in what shape
- Tokenizer recycling is a cheap way to obtain special tokens without the untrained-token security and waste problems
- Multilingual RAG robustness is achievable at 350M, which weakens the assumption that cross-lingual grounding requires a large multilingual encoder

**Relevance**

- The closest published precedent for hypothesis round 4 in `docs/experiments/semantic-grounding-experiments.md`: it is small models, trained for grounding, that emit literal supporting quotes - which is groundrails' own output contract (verdict plus the exact supporting span)
- Establishes the lineage of the R4 candidates - Monad's model card states its grounded-RAG training follows "our initial experiments with Pleias-RAG series", so this paper is the only peer-reviewable technical document behind the round's candidate models
- The refusal-path design and the source-absent evaluation queries map directly onto our task, where the negative class is an unsupported claim rather than a contradicted one
- Caveat for transfer: these are generative RAG answerers scored by an LLM judge on multi-hop QA, not pairwise (claim, evidence) classifiers scored by AUC on a labelled gold. The architecture and training recipe transfer; the benchmark numbers do not
