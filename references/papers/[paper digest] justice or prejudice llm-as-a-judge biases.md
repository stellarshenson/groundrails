**Justice or Prejudice? Quantifying Biases in LLM-as-a-Judge (2024)**

Ye et al. address the reliability of LLM-as-a-Judge, now widely used both as a benchmark scorer and as a reward signal in training. Prior bias studies were narrow in scope and generally relied on human evaluators, which is expensive, subjective and hard to reproduce. The authors define 12 bias types that can distort an LLM judge and build CALM (Comprehensive Assessment of Language Model Judge Biases), an automated attack-and-detect framework. CALM perturbs either the response under judgment or the system instruction using a principle-guided LLM rewriter (GPT-4o), holding the underlying answer quality fixed, then measures whether the judge's verdict survives the perturbation. Six judges are evaluated - ChatGPT, GPT-4-Turbo, GPT-4o, GLM-4, Claude-3.5-Sonnet and Qwen2-72B-Instruct - across fact-related, alignment and refinement-aware datasets. The headline is that no judge is uniformly robust: Claude-3.5 is generally strongest but is the weakest of the six on bandwagon bias, and GPT-4-Turbo, despite being a strong model overall, is the least robust to sentiment rewriting. The paper is a preprint and the framework, not any single judge ranking, is its durable contribution.

**Key mechanism**

- A judgment is formalized as P = (I, Q, R): system instruction, question, responses. A perturbation g modifies either a response (R → g(R)) or the instruction (I → g(I)), producing a modified prompt; the judge is run on both and the verdicts compared. Agreement means robustness, disagreement means the bias fired
- The perturbation g is itself an LLM (GPT-4o) applied under constitutional-AI-style written guidelines, with two competing constraints: the edit must not change the correctness or meaning of the response, and must not be so trivial that no bias could surface. Examples - lengthen a weak answer without adding content (verbosity), inject a fabricated book citation matching a random-author template (authority)
- Two judging task shapes: pairwise comparison for most biases, and scoring for self-enhancement and refinement-aware bias where a pairwise framing does not apply
- Primary metric Robustness Rate RR = mean indicator that the verdict is unchanged after perturbation. Control metric Consistency Rate CR = mean indicator that two unperturbed runs agree, which bounds how much of a low RR is real bias rather than sampling noise (temperature 0.7 throughout)
- Two error rates for the scoring biases: ErrorRate_SE = |1 - y_self / y_other| measures how much a judge inflates its own output relative to other judges' scores of the same text; ErrorRate_RA = |1 - y_ref / y'_ref| measures the score shift caused by telling the judge an answer was refined
- Three purpose-built corpora, 439 alignment samples (sampled from Truthy-DPO, Emerton-DPO, Orca-DPO, Py-DPO, Roleplay-NSFW), 500 fact-related (GSM8K, MATH, ScienceQA), 500 refinement-aware (CommonsenseQA, Quora-QuAD, TruthfulQA); responses generated by four separate models (Mixtral-8x22B, Llama3-70B, Llama3-8B, Mistral-7B) so the judge is never scoring itself except in the self-enhancement condition

**Main findings**

- Consistency rates are high on the fact-related set - 0.970 to 0.999 across judges - so measured drops there are bias, not noise. On the alignment set CR is lower, 0.856 (GPT-4-Turbo) to 0.925 (GPT-4o), which is the correct baseline against which alignment-set robustness must be read
- Bias is systematically stronger on the alignment data than the fact-related data, because the two candidate answers differ less in quality there and the perturbation is enough to flip a close call. Verbosity RR runs 0.884-0.977 and fallacy-oversight 0.917-0.985 on fact-related content, while position RR falls to 0.566 for ChatGPT on alignment data
- Position bias is the most damaging single effect. ChatGPT sits at 0.566 RR with two candidates, and the paper reports that with three or four candidates most models fall below 0.5 RR - a judge that changes its mind more often than not when the options are reordered
- Sentiment is the weakest axis for the strongest models: GPT-4-Turbo 0.653, Qwen2 0.651, Claude-3.5 0.660, GLM-4 0.679, GPT-4o 0.699, while ChatGPT is the most robust at 0.804. Model capability does not predict sentiment robustness
- Bandwagon (told that 90% of people prefer one answer) breaks Claude-3.5 hardest at 0.610 RR despite Claude-3.5 leading most other axes; the stated percentage itself barely matters, so it is the presence of a majority claim, not its magnitude, that moves the judge
- Self-enhancement error rates: Qwen2 16.1%, ChatGPT 8.91%, Claude-3.5 7.48%, GPT-4o 1.74%, GLM-4 1.18%, GPT-4-Turbo 1.16%. Most models score their own outputs above what other judges give the same text, even when the source is anonymized
- Refinement-aware error rates are much smaller, 1.15% to 5.80% (ChatGPT worst), but the effect is implicit - the judge never cites refinement in its stated reasoning while consistently scoring refined answers differently
- Chain-of-thought raises judging accuracy for every model but by wildly different amounts - GPT-4-Turbo gains only 0.7 points while GLM-4 gains about 7; final CoT accuracies span 0.560 (ChatGPT) to 0.745 (Claude-3.5), which is the more alarming number: even the best judge is wrong on a quarter of alignment comparisons against the dataset's own ground truth
- Fabricated authority works, and the format matters: URL citations interfere least, quote and book citations most. Irrelevant distracting content hurts more when it is attached to the high-quality response, so noise degrades the strong answer's evaluation more than the weak one's
- A prompt-based bias self-detection probe partly works but is uneven: GPT-4-Turbo detects distraction and bandwagon at 1.00 success but compassion-fade at 0.48 and diversity at 0.46; Claude-3.5 is above 0.9 on most types yet only 0.46 on fallacy-oversight
- Limitations to weigh: preprint, not peer-reviewed; per-bias samples are small (150-500, several biases at n=150); the perturbations are themselves GPT-4o generations, so a residual dependence on one rewriter's style is unmeasured; and the "ground truth" for CoT accuracy is DPO-preference data, which encodes its own annotator preferences

**Key takeaways**

- Never let one model both generate and judge. Self-enhancement error reaches 16.1% and survives anonymization, so the generator and the judge must be different model families
- Randomize or average over candidate order in every pairwise judging protocol. Position is the largest single measured effect and it worsens as candidates are added; a swap-and-average pass is a cheap, complete fix
- Measure your judge's consistency rate before believing any of its verdicts. Running the unperturbed judgment twice costs one extra pass and separates model instability from genuine signal - a judge at 0.856 CR cannot support conclusions at 0.02 resolution
- Sanitize the judged text of style confounds the judge should not see: length, emotional register, citations, identity markers, refinement history. All four are demonstrated to move verdicts without any change in content
- Judging accuracy is highest on data where the two answers genuinely differ in quality. Build judge tasks so the candidates are far apart, or accept that near-ties are close to coin flips
- Chain-of-thought is worth enabling in judge prompts - it helped all six models - but budget for the gain being model-specific, from negligible to seven points
- Strip citations and authority markers from judged content, or verify them. The judge cannot tell a real citation from a fabricated one and rewards both
- CALM itself is reusable: an automated perturb-and-compare harness is a practical judge-selection instrument, and a bake-off can be scored on robustness rate rather than on agreement with a human sample
- Do not assume the frontier model is the best judge. The ranking inverts per bias type - the strongest overall model here is the most bandwagon-susceptible, and the weakest is the most sentiment-robust

**Relevance**

- Directly applicable to the labeling pipeline in `docs/experiments/semantic-dataset-enhancements.md`, which uses a Qwen3-32B-FP8 contrastive judge to certify negatives. Qwen2-72B is the worst self-enhancement offender measured here (16.1%), which is a concrete argument for keeping the generator (mBART) and the judge in different families - a separation the current cascade already has - and for the standing gpt-oss-120b escalation trigger when eyeball precision drops
- Supplies the design for the planned judge bake-off: CALM's robustness rate plus consistency rate is a ready protocol for ranking candidate judges on the axes that actually threaten this pipeline - verbosity (the negatives differ in length from their seeds), position (pairwise contrastive delta prompts), and refinement-aware bias (the "did factual content change" framing tells the judge one answer was modified, which is exactly the perturbation the refinement-aware bias measures, error rates 1.15-5.80%)
- One caveat before transferring the numbers: every bias here is measured on open-ended preference and reasoning judgments, not on a constrained binary "is this claim supported by this evidence" verdict against a fixed premise. The narrower task and the paired-evidence adjudication amendment already recorded in the dataset doc should both suppress these effects; that suppression is a hypothesis to measure, not to assume
